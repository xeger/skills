# Feedback Conversations

## Overview

The real intention of feedback is growth: helping people see what they cannot see on their own, make meaningful adjustments, and become more effective. When feedback is specific, honest, and rooted in care, it strengthens trust rather than eroding it.

This skill structures feedback along three **channels**, each with its own process:

| Channel | Process | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Requesting** | S.A.F.E. | Build self-awareness; normalize feedback by modeling it |
| **Giving** | C.A.R.E. | Facilitate growth; prevent surprises; leave the person supported |
| **Receiving** | L.E.A.R.N. | Internalize it; make the giver feel heard; name a next step |

The user may be on any channel, and the deliverable is usually a **draft** (message, talking points, or script) plus the reasoning that produced it.

## Requirements

Before producing a draft, you MUST:

1. **Identify the channel.** Requesting, giving, or receiving. If the user is reacting to feedback they got, it's receiving even if they want to "respond."
2. **Run the pre-checks** (below). Trust level and goal alignment change the draft materially.
3. **Get the specifics from the user.** Behavior, situation, and impact must come from them. Never invent an observation, an example, or an impact to make a draft read well.
4. **Apply the channel's process in order.** The acronyms are sequences, not checklists; skipping the first step (set context / check in / listen) is the most common failure.
5. **Keep it to one next step.** A draft that asks for three changes is three drafts.

## Pre-checks

Ask or infer, and state your assumption if inferring:

- **Trust level.** How much trust exists between the two people? Low trust → shorter, more tentative, more permission-seeking; consider whether the feedback should come from someone else or wait.
- **Growth-goal alignment.** Is there a stated growth goal, rock, or development objective this relates to? If yes, anchor to it explicitly. If the feedback *implies* a goal that doesn't exist yet, say so and offer to load `individual-goal-setting` to draft one.
- **Balance.** Is the user giving/requesting only "what needs to change"? "What's working great" feedback should be as frequent or more. If the user's recent feedback has all been corrective, name that.
- **Timing.** Is this timely (close to the event) and part of a frequent cadence, or a surprise? A surprise in a review is a process failure; flag it and suggest the conversation happen sooner and smaller.

## Mindset (apply to every draft)

- **Courageous** – ask, and receive with humility.
- **Curious** – what can I learn from this, even if I don't agree with all of it?
- **Safe and normal** – how does this draft make skillful feedback more ordinary, not less?

Things to remember while drafting:

- Feedback creates fear. Leaders address it by acknowledging it, normalizing it, and modeling frequent requesting.
- Feedback is not *the truth*; it is someone's experience. Drafts should sound like that.
- Behavior and impact, never personality.

## Processes

### Requesting · S.A.F.E.

| Step | What it does | Prompt |
|---|---|---|
| **S** et the context | Tell them what you're working on so they know what to look for | "I'm working on…" |
| **A** sk specifically | A bounded question about a specific moment, not "any feedback?" | "How do you experience me when…?" |
| **F** rame with a scale | 1–10 makes a vague impression quantifiable and makes the next question possible | "On a scale of 1–10, how did I do at…?" |
| **E** xplore and appreciate | Find out what earned the score and what moves it one point | "What helped me earn that? What could move me one point higher?" |

**Script:** "I'm working on being more concise. Thinking about today's meeting, on a scale of 1–10, how effectively did I keep us focused? What should I keep doing, and what could move me one point higher?"

### Giving · C.A.R.E.

| Step | What it does | Prompt |
|---|---|---|
| **C** heck in | Their self-assessment first; they may already see it | "How do you think it went?" |
| **A** sk permission, share observations | Consent, then behavior → impact, in that form | "Can I share something I noticed? When you did ___, the impact was ___." |
| **R** edirect toward the future | Future-focused and action-oriented, owned by them | "What might you try next time?" |
| **E** ncourage and enable | Offer support, verbally and energetically | "How can I help?" |

**Script:** "How do you think that went? Can I share something I noticed? When you did ___, it seemed to ___. What might you try next time? How can I support you?"

### Receiving · L.E.A.R.N.

| Step | What it does | Prompt |
|---|---|---|
| **L** isten and let it land | Pause. Don't explain or defend. | *(silence)* |
| **E** xplore with curiosity | Get an example before judging the feedback | "Can you give me an example?" |
| **A** cknowledge their point of view | Validate the impact without necessarily agreeing with the interpretation | "I can see how that had that impact." |
| **R** eflect and find the 2% truth | Restate it; find the part that is true even if 98% isn't | "What I hear you saying is…" |
| **N** ame the next step | One action, then follow up later | "What I'm taking from this is… Next time, I'll try…" |

**Script:** "Thank you for telling me. Can you give me an example? What I hear you saying is ___. What I'm taking from this is ___. Next time, I'll try ___."

## Workflow

1. **Identify the channel** and the two people's roles (manager → report, peer, report → manager). Role changes the permission step: upward feedback needs more of it.
2. **Run the pre-checks.** Batch the questions.
3. **Collect specifics.** For giving: the behavior, when, and the observed impact. For requesting: what the user is working on and which moment to ask about. For receiving: what was actually said, as close to verbatim as the user has.
4. **Draft using the channel's process**, in sequence, in the user's voice. Mark fill-in-the-blank spots with `___` rather than inventing.
5. **Show the reasoning:** which pre-check findings shaped the draft, and which single next step it lands on.
6. **Offer the follow-up.** Every channel ends with a follow-up; propose when and what to check.

If the user pastes a feedback message and asks for a critique, run Workflow steps 1–2, then evaluate it against the channel's process and the Common Mistakes table, and propose a rewrite labeled as a proposal.

## Examples

### Example 1: Giving — vague frustration → C.A.R.E. draft

- **Input:** "I need to tell a report that their PRs are sloppy."
- **Channel:** Giving, manager → report.
- **Pre-checks:** Trust is established (user says they have a good relationship). Growth goal: none stated → the draft should not invent one; offer goal-setting afterward. Balance: user hasn't given positive PR feedback recently → add a "what's working" line. Timing: last incident was yesterday → timely.
- **Specifics elicited:** Two PRs this week merged with failing lint and no description; reviewers spent ~30 min each reconstructing intent.
- **Draft:**
  > How do you think the last couple of PRs went? Can I share something I noticed? On the two PRs this week, lint was failing and there wasn't a description, and it meant reviewers spent about half an hour each figuring out the intent. Your actual changes were solid, so the work is there. What might you try so the next one lands review-ready? Anything I can set up to help, like a PR template?
- **Next step:** One: PR description + green lint before requesting review. Follow up at the next 1:1.

### Example 2: Receiving — defensive reaction → L.E.A.R.N.

- **Input:** "My manager said I dominate meetings. That's not fair, I'm usually the only one who's prepared."
- **Channel:** Receiving.
- **Pre-checks:** Trust medium. Growth goal: the user has a rock about "raising the team's technical judgment," which is directly relevant → anchor.
- **Draft (for the follow-up conversation):**
  > Thanks for telling me. Can you give me an example of a meeting where that showed up? What I hear you saying is that when I ___, others ___. The 2% I can own is ___. What I'm taking from this is ___. Next time, I'll try ___, which also fits the rock about the team's judgment, since that only works if they're the ones talking.
- **Note to user:** "Only one prepared" may be true and still not be the point. The feedback is about impact on others' participation, which is the user's own goal.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Result | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Skipping the first step (set context / check in / listen) | Feedback lands as an ambush, or the receiver was already going to say it | Always open with context, the other person's self-assessment, or silence |
| Asking "any feedback for me?" | Vague, unanswerable; people say "no, you're great" | Set context, ask about a specific moment, frame 1–10 |
| Personality instead of behavior ("you're careless") | Defensiveness; nothing actionable | "When you did ___, the impact was ___" |
| Impact invented to strengthen the draft | Feedback is now false and the receiver may know it | Only the user's observed impact; `___` if unknown |
| Three asks in one conversation | Nothing changes; the person hears "you're bad at everything" | One next step; park the rest for later conversations |
| Corrective-only feedback diet | Feedback becomes synonymous with criticism; people stop asking | Give and request "what's working" at least as often |
| Explaining or defending while receiving | Giver stops giving feedback; the 2% truth is never found | Listen, explore for an example, acknowledge impact before interpreting |
| Feedback with no growth goal to anchor to | Feels arbitrary; no way to tell if it's working | Link to a stated goal, or offer to draft one with `individual-goal-setting` |
| No follow-up | Loop never closes; neither side knows if it landed | End every draft with a proposed follow-up time and check |

## When NOT to use

- Code review comments or design review feedback on artifacts — those have their own conventions; this skill is about feedback *to a person about their behavior*.
- HR, disciplinary, or legal processes — follow the formal process; this skill may help with tone but does not replace it.
- Compensation or promotion decisions — different conversation, different structure.
